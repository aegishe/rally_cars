Why do trophy trucks use bodies that resemble trucks from well-known brands?
As it turns out, it's not just a matter of design or a love for a particular brand.
In races like the Baja 1000, trophy trucks are subject to rules requiring them to
retain an appearance similar to that of production trucks or SUVs.
However, don't be fooled by their exterior. These vehicles are actually built
almost entirely from scratch using a custom racing tubular frame.
In fact, the body isn't made of factory standard panels, but rather from
lightweight materials like fiberglass or carbon fiber.
Grilles, headlight designs, and body lines characteristic of each manufacturer
make it easy for spectators and fans to recognize.
These truck body regulations serve to distinguish the trophy truck class from
open wheel classes like Class 1 Buggy.
So, even though it looks like a Ford F-150, what you're actually seeing is a
specialized race car that merely borrows the visual identity of a production truck.
